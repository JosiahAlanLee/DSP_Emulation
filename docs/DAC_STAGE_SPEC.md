# DAC stage: period converter character, spec v0.1

Status: **draft contract; early implementation started.** [CZishLab](../vcv/czish)
implements the word formats and a ByteBus XOR/tap experiment. Its raw HOLD,
limited AA, and provisional CZ profile are not the full contract below.

Working names: **DAC Stage** for the
module, `dspemu::dac` for the shared header. Names and slugs are the owner's to
change. Hardware claims cite their source. Items marked *provisional* or
*unverified* still need a primary source or a measurement. The CZ-101 voice that
uses this stage has its own spec, [CZ_VOICE_SPEC.md](CZ_VOICE_SPEC.md).

## 1. Purpose

Part of the 1980s Casio and Yamaha "crunch" comes from the converter rather
than from downsampling:

- word length,
- converters whose quantization step moves with the signal level.

The Yamaha FM chips ran above CD rate (§3.1), and the CZ ran at 40 kHz (§3.2).
How much of an instrument's sound is converter and how much is chip is a
question for measurement; for the CZ that is CZ voice spec §8. Sampler-style
rate crunch is already covered by OTIS.

This stage models the converter half of an instrument:

- the word format,
- the device clock,
- the hold,
- the output filter.

It runs on any Rack signal, including hardware coming in through the ES-6. A
voice emulation running at its chip's own rate can also call the stage directly
(§6).

It does not reproduce what happens inside a chip: table quantization, stepped
envelopes, log/exp arithmetic, or aliasing generated by the oscillator at the
chip's own rate. Those belong to the voice emulations. Running a clean Rack
oscillator through the stage gives you the grain, not the instrument.

## 2. Scope

**v1:**

- Three profiles: **YM FP** (floating-point 10.3, exact word format), **CZ**
  (companded 12-bit, *provisional* until measured) and **LINEAR** (4–16 bits).
- Device clock per profile. RATE is active on YM FP and LINEAR (§4).
- Anti-aliasing switch, OFF by default so the input aliases freely at the
  device clock.
- Two reconstruction modes, HOLD and CLEAN, plus a TONE low-pass.
- Optional analog noise floor, off by default.
- Polyphonic: up to 16 independent channels.

**Deferred:**

- Dither. No source here says whether these converters dithered.
- Op-amp saturation and other analog modelling beyond one low-pass.
- Hum.
- A byte-bus tap (§9, item 4).

## 3. What the sources support

### 3.1 Yamaha floating-point DAC (YM FP)

- **Datasheet.** The [YM3012 datasheet](https://bitsavers.mirrorservice.org/components/yamaha/YM3012_199204.pdf)
  describes a 2-channel serial-input floating D/A converter with a 10-bit
  mantissa and a 3-bit exponent, giving 16-bit dynamic range.
- **Reference code.** [ymfm](https://github.com/aaronsgiles/ymfm) (BSD-3-Clause,
  Aaron Giles; MAME `3rdparty/ymfm` at commit
  [`55784e9`](https://github.com/mamedev/mame/tree/55784e9c9fac41cd7b019f23891c92292af9c679))
  implements the format as `encode_fp`, `decode_fp` and `roundtrip_fp` in
  `ymfm.h` (SHA-256 `947eab9c…5bbe`).
- **Which chips.** `ymfm_opm.cpp` applies `roundtrip_fp` to every output sample
  of the OPM family, YM2151 and its YM2164 (OPP) variant. `ymfm_opz.cpp` does the
  same for the YM2414 (OPZ), with the comment "unsure about YM2414 outputs;
  assume it is like YM2151". The OPZ word format is therefore ymfm's
  assumption, not a documented fact.
- **Which instruments.** MAME's Yamaha drivers, at the same commit:

  | Driver | Chip and clock | Notes |
  |---|---|---|
  | `ymdx100.cpp` | YM2164 at 7.15909 MHz / 2, "with YM3014 DAC" | DX100 and DX27. The header says the OPP was also used in the DX21. |
  | `ymtx81z.cpp` | YM2414 at 7.15909 MHz / 2 | TX81Z |
  | `ymdx11.cpp` | YM2414 at 3.579545 MHz | DX11 |

  The YM3014 is the single-channel counterpart of the YM3012. That it shares
  the 10.3 format is *unverified* here: only the YM3012 datasheet was read. What
  the profile reproduces is the word format.

What `roundtrip_fp` does to a signed 16-bit sample:

- Values with magnitude under 512 (about −36 dBFS) pass exactly.
- Each 6 dB above that doubles the quantization step.
- The step reaches 64 LSB in the top range. That is 10-bit resolution, sign
  included.
- Bits are dropped by masking the two's-complement value. That rounds toward
  −∞ for negative values too (−513 becomes −514), not to nearest.
- Out-of-range input returns −32768 or +32767 directly, without encoding.
  +32767 is not a value the format can hold: the largest code decodes to 32704,
  and an in-range 32767 rounds to 32704. So positive overflow is the one case
  where the round trip and encode-then-decode disagree.
- The exponent is chosen per sample. Within one cycle of a loud wave, the peaks
  are quantized more coarsely than the zero crossings, so the error is
  correlated with the signal rather than sounding like hiss. On a decaying note,
  the grain gets finer in 6 dB steps.

**Device clock.** ymfm runs these chips at clock / 64 (prescale 2, 32 operator
slots per sample). At 3.579545 MHz that is 55,930 Hz. That is the chip clock in
all three drivers above. The default clock is 3.579545 MHz, editable in the
menu.

### 3.2 Casio CZ companding DAC (CZ)

What [Tomás Mulcahy's CZ notes](https://tomasmulcahy.com/?p=320) report:

- The CZ generates a compressed waveform digitally and expands it in the analog
  domain. This lets a 12-bit DAC reach the theoretical equivalent of 14 bits.
- He compares it to dbx noise reduction and the Roland Dimension D. He credits
  expander mis-tracking with the CZ's colour: punchy lows, breathy highs and a
  honky quality.
- A gritty, low-bit-depth grunge that worsens as more notes are held is
  mis-tracking, not normal operation. The fix is the DAC offset adjustment on
  page 59 of the CZ-1 service manual, which he says applies to all CZs.
- The CZ-1's DAC runs at 40 kHz, and its output filter rolls off at 20 kHz.

[Zen Instruments has a page on the same trim for the CZ-1000](http://zeninstruments.blogspot.com/2016/06/cz-1000-dac-adjustment.html).
It could not be fetched for this draft.

What MAME shows, at commit `55784e9`:

- The CZ-101 driver (`src/mame/casio/cz101.cpp`) clocks the µPD933 at
  8.96 MHz / 2. The chip spends 112 clocks per sample (`upd933.h`), so the
  CZ-101 also runs at 40 kHz.
- The driver routes the chip straight to the speaker. MAME models no DAC, no
  compander and no output filter.

**Not established by anything found so far:**

- The compander law. It could be an instantaneous segment law (the gain chosen
  per sample) or syllabic (the gain set by a level detector with time
  constants). The dbx comparison points toward syllabic but doesn't establish
  it.
- Whether companding acts on the summed output or on each voice slot of a
  time-multiplexed DAC. A per-slot design would also explain grunge that grows
  with the note count. This is a hypothesis, not a finding.
- The CZ-101's own output filter. The 20 kHz figure is for the CZ-1.

So the CZ profile ships *provisional* (§5.2) and gets fitted from recordings of
the owner's CZ-101 (CZ voice spec §8).

## 4. Signal flow (per channel)

```
IN → DRIVE → [AA: off | sinc to device rate] → sample at device clock
   → ENCODE (profile) → DECODE → [+ NOISE] → hold
   → [HOLD: band-limited steps | CLEAN: sinc to host] → TONE → LEVEL → OUT
```

**Level.** ±5 V is digital full scale, the repository's byte-bus convention.
DRIVE (−24 to +24 dB) sets how far into the word the signal sits. This matters
most for YM FP, where level decides the exponent. Values past full scale
saturate the way each format does (for YM FP, §3.1). LEVEL is output makeup
gain.

**Sampling.**

- AA OFF (the default): each device-clock instant reads the input by four-point
  Hermite interpolation between host samples. With no band-limiting, content
  above the device Nyquist folds down, as it would at an unfiltered converter
  input.
- AA ON: the input is band-limited to the device rate first by the variable
  converter (§6).
- When the device clock is above the host rate, AA has nothing to remove. The
  two modes are then spectrally almost the same, but AA ON still adds its
  latency.

**Device clock.** A double-precision phase accumulator. RATE applies per
profile:

| Profile | RATE | Range |
|---|---|---|
| YM FP | octaves around clock / 64, knob and CV | ±2 oct |
| CZ | inactive; the clock is fixed | 40 kHz |
| LINEAR | absolute knob, with CV in octaves | 1–96 kHz |

The device clock is capped at 192 kHz. The readout shows Hz.

**Reconstruction.**

- **HOLD** renders the zero-order hold at host rate with band-limited steps.
  The windowed sinc is converted to minimum phase (cepstral method), then
  integrated into a BLEP table. Each device-sample transition is placed at its
  fractional position.
  - Hold images below the host Nyquist come through, which is what an
    unfiltered period output sounds like.
  - Nothing lands on the host grid's timing, so there's no jitter.
  - Images above the host Nyquist are removed.
  - A device clock above the host rate puts every image above the host Nyquist.
    That is YM FP at 55.9 kHz on a 44.1 or 48 kHz host. HOLD then contributes
    only the hold's sin(x)/x droop.
- **CLEAN** converts device to host with the polyphase sinc converter: no
  images.
- **TONE** is a 2-pole low-pass after either mode, OFF at the top of its range.
  The profile sets its default: CZ 20 kHz (*provisional*, from the CZ-1 figure),
  YM FP and LINEAR off.

**Latency.**

- HOLD adds the minimum-phase step's short group delay. It has no fixed
  half-kernel delay.
- CLEAN adds about 32 device samples. That is 0.8 ms at 40 kHz.
- AA ON adds about 32 host samples.
- The dry signal isn't mixed in, so nothing needs delay matching.

**NOISE** adds white noise at the analog side, in dB below full scale, OFF by
default. It is not measured from hardware. It exists because a real CZ-101 has
an audible floor, and its depth is a choice.

## 5. Profiles

### 5.1 YM FP

The 16-bit word is computed by `roundtrip_fp`, ported bit for bit (§8). Floor
rounding, the 10.3 format and the overflow return all follow ymfm. The default
device clock is 3.579545 MHz / 64.

### 5.2 CZ (provisional)

There are two candidate models, selected in the menu, until measurement
chooses one:

- **INSTANT** (default). A 12-bit two's-complement code with a per-sample range
  shift of 0, 1 or 2 bits, giving a 14-bit range. This matches the "12-bit
  reaching 14" description and nothing more.
- **SYLLABIC.** The same 12-bit code and shift range, but the shift is chosen by
  a peak detector with attack and release. Starting values are 1 ms and 50 ms
  (*provisional*). The gain changes slowly, so the noise floor breathes after
  transients.

**TRACK** (menu, default 0) adds a gain error per range step on the expansion
side, in percent. This is the mis-tracked, untrimmed CZ as a deliberate option.
It is modelled from Mulcahy's description, not measured.

Mulcahy credits mis-tracking with the CZ's normal colour as well as with the
fault. TRACK at 0 is an ideal 12+2 quantizer, not a trimmed CZ. The trimmed
behaviour comes from the measurement. If that shows something different, TRACK
is redefined or dropped.

The device clock is fixed at 40 kHz. TONE defaults to 20 kHz.

### 5.3 LINEAR

- BITS 4–16, with CV. Fractional CV values are rounded, and the readout shows
  the active bit count.
- Floor rounding by default, round-to-nearest in the menu.
- RATE is free from 1 kHz to 96 kHz, defaulting to the host rate (§4).

This is the generic word-length and rate reducer. It covers the 8- and 12-bit
samplers without claiming any of them.

## 6. Shared code

`vcv/shared/DacStage.hpp`: BSD-3-Clause, no Rack dependency, no allocation in
`process`.

```cpp
namespace dspemu::dac {
int16_t ymRoundTrip(int32_t v);            // ymfm roundtrip_fp, bit-exact
int16_t linearRoundTrip(int32_t v, int bits, bool round);
struct CzCompander { /* INSTANT | SYLLABIC, TRACK; process(int32_t) -> float */ };

enum class CzModel { Instant, Syllabic };
struct Settings {
    Profile profile; double deviceRate, ymClockHz; int bits; bool roundNearest;
    CzModel czModel; float czTrackPct; bool aa, hold;
    float driveDb, toneHz, noiseDb, levelDb;
};
struct Stage {                              // host-rate entry: any Rack signal
    void configure(double hostRate, const Settings&);   // not real-time safe
    float process(float in);
};
struct DeviceOutput {                       // device-rate entry: a voice at its chip rate
    void configure(double deviceRate, double hostRate, const Settings&);
    void push(int32_t chipSample);          // once per device sample
    float read();                           // once per host sample
};
}
```

**Two entry points.** A voice that already runs at its chip's rate, such as the
CZ voice at 40 kHz or a future OPZ voice at 55.9 kHz, feeds its integer samples
to `DeviceOutput`. That skips the input sampling stage and keeps the chip's own
aliasing. Everything else uses `Stage`.

**Rate converters.** New shared code goes in `vcv/shared/RateConverter.hpp`.
There are two kinds, because RATE moves:

- **Fixed ratio.** This is a third copy of the OTIS/Spoon Bender polyphase
  converter: 64 taps, 1024 phases, Blackman. Its `configure` allocates and is
  not real-time safe, so it serves rates that only change from the menu: CZ at
  40 kHz, and CLEAN output at a fixed device rate.
- **Variable ratio.** This follows OTIS's `LabConverter` design: 64 taps, 512
  phases, 9 precomputed cutoff buckets from 0.46 down to 0.115 of the input
  rate. RATE CV moves between buckets without reallocating.
  - Below the lowest bucket's 4:1 ratio, AA ON first decimates with a cascade
    of half-band 2× stages. LINEAR at 1 kHz from a 48 kHz host is one such case.
- **Existing copies stay.** OTIS and Spoon Bender keep their own. Spoon Bender
  copied rather than shared so OTIS's bit-exact traces stay untouched, and the
  same reason applies here.

## 8. Testing

- **YM FP parity.** `ymRoundTrip` against an excerpt of ymfm's `roundtrip_fp`
  at the pinned commit, exhaustive over all 16-bit inputs plus overflow on both
  sides.
- **YM FP format.** For in-range inputs, `encode_fp` followed by `decode_fp`
  equals the round trip. Positive overflow is excluded (§3.1). The step size
  equals 2^(exponent − 1) in each range.
- **LINEAR.** Step sizes per bit count. Floor and round each match their
  definitions, including negative values.
- **Clock.** Device samples per second equal the set rate within ±1 sample over
  10 s, at host rates of 44.1, 48 and 96 kHz.
- **Aliasing.**
  - AA OFF: a sine above the device Nyquist appears at its folded frequency.
  - AA ON: that same sine is suppressed by the converter's stopband. This is
    tested at host-to-device ratios of 1, 2, 4 and 48 to 1, which covers the
    half-band cascade.
- **HOLD.** With a device clock below the host rate (CZ at 40 kHz on a 48 kHz
  host; LINEAR at 16 kHz), the image spectrum below the host Nyquist matches the
  analytic ZOH response, sin(x)/x, within 0.5 dB. No component appears that
  isn't on the folded image grid, so the timing is jitter-free. With YM FP above
  the host rate, only the passband droop is checked.
- **Transparency.** LINEAR 16-bit, device rate equal to host rate, CLEAN, AA ON,
  with the output delay-aligned to the input: error below −90 dB up to 12 kHz.
  The two 0.46 fs kernels in series roll off above that.
- **CZ provisional.** Only the invariants: range selection, and TRACK at 0 being
  exact against an ideal 12+2 quantizer. Matching hardware is the measurement's
  job.
- **Rack module.** Random module order, poly channel counts 1, 8 and 16, at
  44.1, 48 and 96 kHz, under ASan/UBSan, as the other plugins do.

## 9. Open decisions

1. **Home.** Is DAC Stage a second module in the CZ voice's plugin, or its own
   plugin? Proposal: a second module there. The CZ profile is fitted from that
   work, and the shared header keeps it usable by everything else.
2. **Ship CZ as provisional, or hide it until fitted.** Proposal: ship it
   labelled provisional in the menu and manual.
3. **The shared rate converters** in `vcv/shared` (§6): a third copy of the
   fixed one, and a variable one on the LabConverter design.
4. **Byte-bus tap.** A Harriot BUS OUT carrying the top 8 bits of each device
   word, clocked at the device rate when it's under the bus limit. This is a
   v1.1 candidate.
5. **TRACK.** Keep it as a deliberate option, or drop it once the trim is
   understood.
