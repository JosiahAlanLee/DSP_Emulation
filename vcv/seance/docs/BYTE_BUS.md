# Séance Bridge: spectrum and byte bus

Contract v1 · SeanceLab 2.1.0 · original expander · 8 HP

Bridge sits anywhere in the unbroken Séance row. It measures the spectral frame
at its position, publishes eight activity bits, then applies an incoming
eight-bit band mask to that same frame. Earlier expanders affect the measurement;
later ones affect the masked result. It uses the existing analysis/resynthesis
pair and adds no FFT or audio latency. The frame stays inside the Séance row.

## Bands

Channel 1 is bit 0, the lowest band. Bins are assigned by their centre frequency;
each bin belongs to exactly one band. A boundary belongs to the higher band.
DC belongs to bit 0 and Nyquist to bit 7 (or the band containing it at low sample
rates). Bands above Nyquist are empty. Coarse FFT sizes can leave low bands
empty too: Bridge does not invent frequency resolution.

| Bit | Frequency at ordinary audio sample rates |
|---|---|
| 0 | DC–125 Hz |
| 1 | 125–250 Hz |
| 2 | 250–500 Hz |
| 3 | 500 Hz–1 kHz |
| 4 | 1–2 kHz |
| 5 | 2–4 kHz |
| 6 | 4–8 kHz |
| 7 | 8 kHz–Nyquist |

## BUS IN: band mask

The shared [byte-bus protocol](../../../docs/BYTE_BUS_SPEC.md) applies: eight
LSB-first 0/10 V data lines; channel 9, when present, is CLK. Inputs use the
shared Schmitt reader; missing bits are zero and mono is one bit, not broadcast.
Channels 10–16 are ignored.

- Unconnected: all bands open, target mask `FF`.
- 1–8 channels: current decoded word is the target mask.
- 9–16 channels: latch on each bus rising edge and hold indefinitely, even with
  a stopped clock. A new clocked connection starts at `00` until its first edge;
  an initially high CLK counts as that edge.
- The most recent held word is sampled **when Bridge receives a spectral
  frame**, not at the core's earlier analysis instant. Several updates before a
  frame coalesce into the latest. Each bit set opens its band; a cleared bit
  closes it. Data and CLK still travel together on one cable.
- **SLEW**, 0–250 ms (default 20 ms), is an exponential band-gain time constant.
  Once per frame, `gain += (target - gain) * (1-exp(-hop/(rate*tau)))`.
  Zero applies the target immediately. Gains start open. Disconnecting also
  returns toward open using SLEW. Overlap-add windows add their own transition
  spread; SLEW does not guarantee click-free changes.
  Gains within one millionth of their target snap to it, avoiding denormals.

Only magnitudes are multiplied; phases and B's reference spectrum are unchanged.
BUS IN never clocks the FFT, RESET, Freeze or Scramble. Ordinary CV ports retain
their existing behavior.

## BUS OUT: activity

BUS OUT always emits **nine channels**. Each new frame publishes the activity
word with a **one-engine-sample 10 V clock pulse** on channel 9. Between frames
the word holds and CLK is zero; identical successive words still get clocks.
This clock is generated by the spectral hop, independent of BUS IN's clock.
It is not an onset detector or a musical beat clock.

Activity is measured **before Bridge applies its own mask**. Closing every band
does not make its own detector deaf. Upstream Wipe/Freeze/Scramble/Cross still
affect what Bridge hears; placing another Bridge later measures the result.

For each band, power is the sum of squared FFT magnitudes, with a factor of two
for interior bins and one for DC/Nyquist, divided by `N*N*3/8`. This compensates
for the core's Hann window. The square root estimates band RMS in the core's
units, where 1 is 10 V. For transformed spectra it describes that spectral
frame, not a meter of the final time-domain output.

- **THRESH**, −72–0 dBFS (default −36): a low bit turns on at or above this
  band RMS level. 0 dBFS is 10 V RMS; −36 dBFS is about 0.158 V RMS.
- **HYST**, 0–24 dB (default 6): a high bit turns off at or below
  `THRESH − HYST`. With zero hysteresis, the single comparison is `>= THRESH`.
- Silence and empty bands are always low. Invalid magnitudes contribute no
  power. There is no normalization to the loudest band, automatic gain control,
  or artificial activity in silence.

At the default 2048-point FFT and 48 kHz, the hop is 512 samples: 93.75 words/s.
The existing core audio latency is 2111 samples. The activity clock marks the
frame being processed, not when that frame's audio becomes audible. Clocked
receivers can use it directly; Register's Internal mode can sample more slowly.

## Lifecycle, bypass and saving

Before the first frame and while unlinked or bypassed, output is clocked `00`
with CLK low. Bypass passes the spectral frame untouched and clears Bridge's
transient state. Relinking to another core, changing frame size or sample rate,
or resetting Bridge clears activity and starts gains open. Core RESET restarts
its frame grid as before; Bridge continues to measure the subsequent frames.

Knobs save with the Rack patch. Input latch, activity bits, band gains and frame
contents do not. Loading starts from the lifecycle state above. Original core
and four expander parameter/port IDs and their frame format are unchanged.

## Feedback example

Open [`../examples/OTIS-spectral-feedback.vcv`](../examples/OTIS-spectral-feedback.vcv),
select an audio device and press OTIS **PLAY**. No sample download is needed.
OTIS's original embedded frame bank feeds Séance. Bridge activity enters an
internally clocked Register; Lookup transforms it into a mask and an OTIS frame
selection. The two branches share the same word. Bridge's detector remains
upstream of its own mask. A second output connection lets you hear OTIS dry
beside the processed Séance signal. The row is mono; this is not stereo spectral
processing.

Register controls the feedback update rate. Try another Lookup shape or family,
THRESH, HYST and SLEW, or put Scramble between the core and Bridge. An unchanging
word or repeating cycle can be a valid stable state of this patch; it is not
guaranteed to keep evolving.
